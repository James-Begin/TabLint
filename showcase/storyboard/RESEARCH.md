## Direction drawn from the references

A good product demonstration makes the interaction legible: open something recognizable, invoke the tool, inspect its output, choose an action, and see what changed. The references above support a real workspace and a clear action/result structure. Our row swoops, lifted tokens, shallow camera orbits, and match cuts are original creative choices for this film, not claimed observations of those clips.

The previous TabLint video moved flattened SVG panels. That creates camera motion without product motion. This new storyboard has individually addressable focus feedback, CSV, tooltip, menu, field, context, and ledger layers. The motion study previews their timing; final production uses the same event-driven structure.

## Design implications

- Keep one light VS Code desktop as the spatial anchor.
- Camera motion connects interactions; it settles when the reason needs reading.
- Let rows and category values lift out of the CSV to explain the model's context, then return to their source.
- Tie every activation to a visible UI change. A fix retracts a warning and adds a ledger event.
- Spend the most time on a human decision, not on branding or unverified model statistics.
- Finish by pulling out from the corrected workspace into the brand composition.
